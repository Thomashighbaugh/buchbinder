# Author Combinations Variations


A companion to `76-Authors-Variations.md`. The same Snow White blurb is retold through **blends of three authors** instead of one, as a quick reference for asking an LLM to write in a combined voice. Each entry names the three authors by surname and gives one sentence assigning a role and a weight to each voice:

- **The first author** supplies the **narrative voice**: who is telling it, from where, and with what attitude. This carries the largest weight.
- **The second author** supplies the **thematic focus**: what the retelling is really about.
- **The third author** supplies the **writing style**: sentence shape, diction, and surface texture.

The weights are rough proportions of influence, not measurements. As with the source file, these are reconstructions by an LLM (Claude Opus 5.5), not snapshots of any author's actual prose. The combinations draw on the same idiosyncratic list of 76 authors. Dialogue uses straight double quotes throughout, except where an author's signature is to drop quotation marks altogether.

## 1. Thompson / Kerouac / Wilde

*Hunter S. Thompson (50%) with the thematic focus of Jack Kerouac (30%) and the writing style of Oscar Wilde (20%).*

>
> Every morning the Queen stood before the mirror like a junkie before the only dealer in town who still took her credit. "Who is the most beautiful woman in the land?" she asked, and the glass, which had learned long ago that flattery is the only honest trade in a palace, said, "You are, Majesty. You are the fairest of them all." It was the same question every day and the same answer every day, and repetition, as any sensible person knows, is merely boredom that has learned to dress well.
>
> But the whole rotten machine was running on borrowed time, the way a car on the open road runs on fumes and faith, and one grey morning the mirror broke the ritual. "You are beautiful," it said, "but Snow White is more beautiful. She has youth, Majesty, and youth is the only thing that cannot be bought, only spent."
>
> The Queen felt the floor tilt under her like a highway at three in the morning. Somewhere out there the girl was moving, alive, burning like a roman candle, and the Queen was standing still. She sent for a huntsman, a large silent brute with the moral flexibility of a wet rope, and told him to take Snow White deep into the woods and kill her. "One should never compete with the young," she said, pouring a glass of something red. "One should simply arrange for them to stop."
>

## 2. McCarthy / Tolkien / Hemingway

>
> The queen stood each morning before the glass in the high cold room where the light came grey through the lancets. She asked it who was fairest in all the land and it told her. You are, it said. It was the answer it had always given and the answer she had come for and it was enough.

## 3. Austen / Dostoevsky / Wilde

*Jane Austen (50%) with the thematic focus of Fyodor Dostoevsky (30%) and the writing style of Oscar Wilde (20%).*

>
> It is a truth seldom acknowledged in palaces that a woman who asks the same question every morning is seeking not information but absolution. Every day the Queen stood before her looking-glass and inquired, "Who is the most beautiful woman in the land?" and every day the glass, being well-bred, replied, "You are the most beautiful of all." Her Majesty professed to find the ceremony tedious; she would have been wretched without it. Vanity, like debt, is a burden one grows fond of carrying.
>
> But one morning the mirror, in a fit of candour quite unsuited to furniture, allowed that while the Queen was undoubtedly handsome, Snow White was a great deal more so. The Queen did not faint; she had too much pride for that, and pride is only suffering that has learned good posture. She sat a long while with her hands in her lap, examining a feeling she would have called injustice and which a less interested observer might have called envy.
>
> By evening she had resolved it, as the proud always resolve their torments, by handing them to someone else. She summoned one of her huntsmen and instructed him, in the same even voice she used for ordering dinner, to take Snow White into the wood and kill her.
>

## 4. Pratchett / Orwell / Dickens

> Every morning the Queen stood in front of her magic mirror and asked it who was the most beautiful woman in the land, and every morning the mirror said, "You are," because it had been enchanted to tell the truth and had quickly worked out that in a palace the truth is whatever keeps you off the fire. The Ministry of Reflections, as the court privately called it, had never once been wrong. The huntsman, who had a family, said, "Yes, ma'am." That is how most terrible things get done: by people who have families and say yes, ma'am.

## 13. Melville / Conrad / Milton

## 17. Pynchon / Dick / Wallace
